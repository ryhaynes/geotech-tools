References & Method Notes

Method A (fully mobilized, effective stress β-method). The unfactored downward force curve is built from the sustained dead load, Qd, adding accumulated negative skin friction (fn = β·σ′v) down the pile. The unfactored upward force curve is built from the ultimate toe resistance, Rt, adding accumulated positive shaft resistance (fs = β·σ′v) up from the toe. Their intersection is the neutral plane. This mirrors Fellenius's Unified Method Figure 2A / 3A construction (Fellenius, 2025) and is the basis for the Siegel et al. (2013) LRFD "Unified Design" procedure adopted into FHWA-NHI-16-009.

Downward: Fd(z) = Qd + Σ0→z per·β·σ′v·Δz
Upward: Fu(z) = Rt + ΣL→z per·β·σ′v·Δz
Neutral Plane: depth zn where Fd(zn) = Fu(zn); Qn = Fd(zn) − Qd

Mixed β / presumptive-αb profiles. Each stratum independently selects its unit-resistance method. β-method layers scale with effective stress (σ′v) as depth and groundwater conditions change; presumptive αb layers use a fixed AASHTO Table 10.9.3.5.2-1 value regardless of depth, matching how the project's micropile bond calculators are built. Mixing methods within one profile is intentional and common — e.g., an overburden soil profile in β-method above a rock socket entered as presumptive αb.

What this tool does not do: it does not run a Method B / t-z-q-z settlement-compatibility loop (Fellenius's "Loop" procedure, Fig. 3), does not compute settlement below the pile toe (equivalent-raft method), and does not perform pile-group interior/perimeter load distribution (Franke principle). Those remain hand/software analyses per the referenced literature.
