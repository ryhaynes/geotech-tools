Two N-based correlations, one per soil class: coarse-grained (granular) samples return a friction angle φ from Peck, Hanson & Thornburn (1974); fine-grained (cohesive) samples return an undrained shear strength Su = (N1)60 / 7.5 ksf. Per the source spreadsheet convention, the overburden correction CN is set to 1.0 for fine-grained soils, so (N1)60 = N₆₀ for those samples.
Optional third correlation — drained φ′ from Plasticity Index (fine-grained only): for a fine-grained sample, entering a PI (%) value in its row unlocks an independent estimate of drained friction angle φ′, from the digitized trend line of Terzaghi, Peck & Mesri (1996), Soil Mechanics in Engineering Practice, Fig. 19.7 — this tool's own rendering of the published plate, not a reproduction of it. It runs entirely off Plasticity Index, independent of N-value/(N₁)₆₀, so a fine-grained sample can carry both an undrained Su (N-based) and a drained φ′ (PI-based) side by side — useful since undrained and drained parameters govern different analyses (e.g. short-term bearing capacity vs. long-term slope stability). The PI field is greyed out until a fine-grained stratum is assigned, and is valid for PI ≤ 200% (samples above that are flagged — consult the reference plate directly for shales / clay minerals in that range). Once any sample has PI entered, φ′(PI) results appear alongside Su throughout the tool — the sample row, the boring Results table and pills, the friction-angle profile plot, the project Summary, and a dedicated φ′ vs. PI reference chart comparing your samples to the published trend.
